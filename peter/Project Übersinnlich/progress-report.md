## PRODUCTION NOTES:

- **Vocals:** Chris Cornell rangevulnerable low register in verses, signature high anguished wails in choruses. Voice should crack intentionally in final chorus showing breakdown
- **Guitars:** Kim Thayil approachde-tuned Drop D, dissonant voicings, Eastern modal influences in solo. Clean in verses, crushing in choruses
- **Bass:** Heavy, prominent, Ben Shepherd-style. Melodic underneath the chaos
- **Drums:** Matt Cameron dynamicsquiet restraint in verses, tribal powerful in choruses, time signature shift in bridge (7/8)
- **Bridge:** Critical sectionstrip to bass/drums for spoken/raved section, build back to explosive release with dissonant solo
- **Lovecraft Syllables:** Introduce fragments early, build to full chant in final chorus. Make them melodic but Wrong
- **Production:** Raw Soundgarden aesthetic. Room sound, organic. Extreme quiet/loud dynamics

**Thematic Core:** The chosen become the broken. The SS candidate represents the true believers in Nazi ideology who volunteered for Vril enhancement, expecting to become the Übermensch. Instead, they experience visceral psychological and physical breakdown"breakthroughs" that are actually apertures being opened in human consciousness for forces that don't care about human ambitions.

Unlike previous characters:
- **Kessler:** Sought power, became possessed
- **Al-Fezzani:** Warned, was martyred
- **Brandt:** Transported, corroded by guilt
- **Wiligut:** Mystical, sanctified doom
- **Richter:** Commander, resigned to fate
- **Vogel:** Worker, witnessing collapse

**SS Candidate:** Willing volunteer, experiencing the horror directly in their body and mind. They wanted transcendence and got disintegration. The synchronized nightmares, shared Lovecraft syllables, nosebleeds, poltergeist effectsall clinically documented by Dr. Metzger as "progress" while the candidates experience it as dissolution of self.

*"They chose me for my bloodline and my faith"*  *"We're not transcending, we're becoming doors"*  *"We were never the vessels, we were the door"*

**Body Horror Through Soundgarden's Lens:** Cornell's voice can express both fragility and power, perfect for depicting someone who thought they were strong enough to handle cosmic forces and discovering they're not. The extreme dynamics mirror psychological statequiet vulnerability in verses (initial symptoms)  explosive chorus anguish (full breakdown). The Lovecraft syllables become a melodic element, something that sounds almost beautiful but is fundamentally Wrong.

**Differentiation:** More visceral and immediate than previous songs. Pearl Jam showed working-class dignity; Soundgarden shows chosen elite crumbling. The candidate isn't just witnessing horrorthey're becoming part of it, their body a laboratory for Metzger's experiments and a doorway for ancient forces.

---

**Musical Context - Why Soundgarden:**

Soundgarden perfected making darkness soar. Chris Cornell could take subjects like depression ("Fell on Black Days"), alienation ("Black Hole Sun"), and madness ("The Day I Tried to Live") and give them anguished beauty through his four-octave range. Here, that aesthetic translates cosmic body horror into something simultaneously beautiful and terrifyingthe candidate's breakdown is awful, but Cornell's voice would make it transcendent.

Kim Thayil's dissonant, Eastern-influenced guitar work adds psychedelic wrongness perfect for depicting geometry that shouldn't exist, mathematics bleeding through into consciousness. His solos aren't technical displaysthey're emotional expressions of the inexpressible.

The 7/8 time signature in the bridge creates rhythmic disorientation matching mental state. Soundgarden frequently used odd meters (6/8 in "Spoonman," shifting meters in "My Wave") making them perfect for depicting consciousness coming unmoored.

**The Candidate's Arc:** Faith  Symptoms  Breakdown  Realization  Dissolution. By the outro, they understand they were never meant to contain Vril energythey were meant to be opened by it. Dr. Metzger's clinical "Excellent progress" against their whispered "We were the door" creates final horror: their suffering is exactly what was intended.
